ABOUT

Scientist, researcher,
curious about life.

I am a biologist and postdoctoral researcher at Kiel University. My research focuses on how fungi adapt to their hosts and environments. I work with a broad range of fungi from diverse ecological niches and hosts: plant pathogenic, soil saprotrophic, and entomopathogenic fungi.

Fungi's remarkable ecological diversity and genomic plasticity continue to motivate my scientific curiosity. Through my research, I hope to advance our understanding of these organisms, which have profoundly impacted, and continue to impact (both positively and negatively), medicine, biotechnology, industry, and agriculture.

Experience & education

CURRENT

Postdoctoral Researcher

Christian-Albrechts-Universität zu Kiel (CAU), Germany

I am currently a member of the Fungal Evolutionary Genetics group (https://fungal-evolutionary-genetics.de/the-team/), led by Dr. Michael Habig, where I use population and comparative genomics to study genetic determinants that underlie host adaptation in the genus

Metarhizium.

PAST

Doctor of Sciences (PhD)

ETH Zürich (Swiss Federal Institute of Technology Zurich)

My PhD thesis, conducted in the Plant Pathology group under the supervision of Prof. Bruce McDonald, Dr. Alice Feurtey, and Dr. Cécile Lorrain, is entitled “Adaptation and Resilience Mechanisms of

Zymoseptoria tritici

Under Environmental Stress.” This research generated valuable insights into the phenotypic responses and potential molecular mechanisms that enable

Z. tritici

to thrive under adverse environmental conditions.

PAST

Master of Science (MSc) in Evolutionary Biology

double title from Uppsala University and the University of Montpellier

During the Erasmus Mundus Masters' Programme in Evolutionary Biology (MEME), I undertook two MSc thesis projects. The first focused on predicting Nod-Like Receptors (NLRs) and detecting balancing selection in Sordariales genomes, under the supervision of Dr. Pierre Gladieux at the Plant Health Institute of Montpellier (PHIM), France. The second explored comparative genomics of Helotiales fungi under the supervision of Prof. Jan Stenlid at the Swedish University of Agricultural Sciences (SLU), Sweden.
